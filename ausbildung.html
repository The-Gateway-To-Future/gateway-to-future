Why Choose Ausbildung?

Unlike standard university degrees, the German Ausbildung (dual vocational system) combines classroom theory (30%) with paid, on-the-job training (70%). It is the fastest, lowest-cost bridge to build a high-paying, certified career in Germany.

🎓

Dual System Framework

Alternating weeks between vocational school (Berufsschule) and the workplace.

💼

No Sperrkonto Required

If your monthly stipend exceeds €939, you do NOT need a €11,904 blocked account.

The Dual Education System Explained

In Germany, vocational education is highly respected, regulated by the Federal Ministry, and integrated directly with the national industry. Here is how your schedule splits between theory and practice:

30%

Vocational School (Berufsschule)

You spend 1 to 2 days per week (or in blocks of weeks) studying the theoretical aspects of your chosen trade, German professional terminology, business economics, and civic studies.

70%

Hands-on Training (Ausbildungsbetrieb)

You work 3 to 4 days per week in a real company under the strict supervision of a certified master trainer (Ausbilder). You learn by doing, on the production line, office, or hospital floor.

100%

Integrated Path to PR

After graduating, you get an immediate 12-month job seeker visa. Because you paid social contributions during training, you qualify for permanent residency in Germany in just 2 years.

Since most of our partner programs (like Nursing, IT, and Masons) pay €1,000 to €1,400+ per month from the first year, you are completely exempt from the blocked account requirement. If a program pays less, you only need to block the deficit, not the full €11,904.

Yes, family reunion (Ehegattennachzug) is legally possible. However, you must prove that you can support your spouse without relying on social security (which requires adequate apartment space and additional joint income or proof of savings). We recommend starting your training first and applying for family reunion after successfully passing your probation period (usually 4 months).

If you fail the final exams (Abschlussprüfung) at the Chamber of Commerce (IHK) or Chamber of Crafts (HWK), German law protects you. You are entitled to request an extension of your contract and training period with your employer. You can retake the exam up to two times while continuing to receive your monthly stipend. The success rates are very high, and our team provides tutoring support.

Legally, there is no upper age limit for vocational training in Germany. However, for visa approval in India and employer interest, the sweet spot is between 18 and 35. If you are older than 30, having relevant professional background or academic diplomas in India helps justify the career change to embassy officials.

For high-interaction fields like Generalist Nursing and Office Management, B2 is mandatory to understand clinical patient needs or client documentation. For engineering, IT, and skilled trades (like carpentry or bricklaying), B1 is often accepted by employers, and sometimes even A2 is legally sufficient if the company conducts training in a supportive format. We always recommend aiming for B1/B2 to secure the best placements.
